Privacy Policy

Pip's Code Quest · Last updated: October 9, 2026

The short version: there are no accounts, and we never ask for a child's personal information. Progress stays on the device. Ads are shown in child-safe mode, with no ad tracking and no personalized ads.

What the app stores

Learning progress (lessons finished, stars, streaks, badges, settings) and the first name a child types in. This stays on the device and is never sent to us. We don't run any servers that collect it. Deleting the app deletes it.

Advertising

The free version shows a small banner ad from Google AdMob on the main screens, never during lessons. Every ad request is marked as directed to children under the US Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) and as coming from a user under the age of consent. That means:

  • No personalized or interest-based ads, and no remarketing.
  • No use of the advertising identifier (IDFA). The app never asks permission to track.
  • Only ads rated suitable for general audiences.

To deliver and count ads and to prevent fraud, Google may still process limited technical information such as the device's IP address, device type and app interactions with the ad. See how Google uses information from apps that use its services and Google's child-directed ad settings.

Purchases

"Remove ads" is a one-time purchase handled entirely by Apple. We never see payment details. Purchases sit behind a grown-up check.

Reminders

If allowed, the app schedules a daily practice reminder on the device itself. No notification data is sent anywhere. Reminders can be turned off in the app's grown-up area or in iPhone Settings.

Parents' rights

Because we don't collect personal information from children, we have none to review or delete. A parent can erase all progress at any time with "Start over" in the grown-up area, or by deleting the app. Questions? Email sagarika125@gmail.com.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update the date above. If we ever want to collect new kinds of information, we'll ask for a parent's consent first.
